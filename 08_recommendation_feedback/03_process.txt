Module: Recommendation and Feedback
Process and techniques

The recommendation is the final product shown to the user. It must be clear, useful, and honest about uncertainty. The user should quickly understand whether to finalize the build, swap a component, wait for a price drop, or avoid the combination.

Recommended response fields
- action_code, compatibility_status, and confidence
- short_summary and immediate_actions (e.g. "confirm PSU wattage before checkout")
- primary_build and alternatives with trade-offs
- setup_instructions and manufacturer_support_contacts
- reasons and source citations
- observed_at, fetched_at, and expires_at
- limitations and degraded_services
- request_id and version metadata

Key techniques
- Use short action sentences and place urgent steps first (e.g. a wattage warning).
- Use alert deduplication and cooldown to reduce notification fatigue for price-drop alerts.
- Never hide an alert when a part becomes out of stock or discontinued.
- Classify feedback as helpful, incorrect, stale, unsafe, price issue, or source issue.
- Sanitize, label, and review feedback before using it for model training.
- Measure safety metrics such as incompatible-build recall, unsafe recommendation rate, and alert latency.
- Measure user-experience metrics separately.
- Use canary or A/B tests only for presentation changes, not for weaker safety thresholds.
- Apply data minimization, access control, retention, export, and deletion rules to purchase history.

Success means the advice is timely, supported by evidence, easy to act on, and connected to a controlled improvement process.
